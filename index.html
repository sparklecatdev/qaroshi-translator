FIELD NOTES

How Qaroshi speaks.

A tiny reference for reading translations, building sentences, and understanding what the app can actually verify.

01

The resonance comes first

Written independent sentences begin with one of 16 resonance markers. sen is neutral; më is tender, and hë is inquisitive.

sen mi haro.I am well.
02

Sentence order

Ordinary declarative clauses use subject–object–verb ordering. Nouns carry their roles as case endings, not through English-style word order.

sen mi naro-m daru.I protect the home.
03

Cases carry meaning

-m marks direct objects; -tu recipients; -gu goals; -lo locations; -za passive agents.

sen mi mara-tu kima-m pëli.I give water to a friend.
04

Verbs keep their shape

After a verb root, -te refers to past events and -yu to future ones. -ki, -ho, and -du distinguish ability, permission, and obligation.

sen sa goro-yu.They will fly.
05

Translation is not guessing

Exact source matches are documented examples. New sentences assembled from grammar are labeled provisional. If a meaning or construction isn't supported, the app says so rather than inventing a confident answer.

nëgëra ≠ ngënao ... nuNot all is different from none.
